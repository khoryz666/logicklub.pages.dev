Why can I only drag two points on the graph?

In 2D space, everything is built from two "building blocks" called Basis Vectors: a right arrow (red) and an up arrow (blue).

A matrix transformation keeps grid lines perfectly straight and parallel. Because of this, if we know where those two arrows land, we instantly know where every single point on the grid goes!

Drag the red point: You change the first column of the matrix (the horizontal rule).
Drag the blue point: You change the second column of the matrix (the vertical rule).

Quick Terms:
Matrix: The grid of numbers that defines the warping rule.
Column: Tells us where one basis arrow lands.

Real-World AI Applications

How does AI actually use this space-warping math?

Computer Vision

Computers see images as giant grids of numbers (matrices). To recognize a cat even if the picture is tilted, the AI applies Matrix Transformations to mathematically rotate or scale the image—just like you did in the visualizer!

Neural Networks

When an AI learns to separate "Cats" from "Dogs", it plots them as points in space. "Learning" is simply the AI finding the perfect matrix to warp that space until the cats and dogs are neatly separated on opposite sides!

How exactly does this relate to Neural Networks?

A Neural Network is made of multiple layers. Each layer applies a matrix transformation to the data—stretching and rotating it in high-dimensional space just like the graph above.

At first, these matrices contain random numbers (called weights). As the AI trains on examples, it slowly turns these "number knobs", tweaking the matrix until the data is perfectly separated into correct categories.
